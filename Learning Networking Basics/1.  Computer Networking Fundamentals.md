## Topic 1 — What is a Network and How Do Devices Communicate?

A network is simply two or more devices connected in a way that allows them
to send and receive data. At its largest scale, the internet is just a 
massive network of smaller networks all interconnected.

---
### The Postal Analogy

The mailing system is a great way to think about this:

- **Data** is broken into small chunks called **packets** — like splitting a
  large shipment into labeled boxes so they're easier to move and reroute.
  If one box gets lost, only that one needs to be resent.
- The **router** is like a regional postal hub — it sits between neighborhoods
  (networks) and figures out how to get your mail to the right city. It
  connects different networks together, including getting your traffic out
  to the internet and back.
- The **switch** is like the local mail sorter inside your neighborhood — it
  takes incoming packages and figures out which house (device) on the local
  network to deliver them to. *(More on how it identifies houses in Topic 3.)*
- The **device (house)** receives all the packages and reassembles them. If
  something didn't arrive, it can request just that missing piece.

---
### Device Roles at a Glance

| Device         | Role                                            | Analogy                        |
| -------------- | ----------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| **End device** | Sends and receives data                         | A house                        |
| **Switch**     | Moves data between devices on the same network  | Local neighborhood mail sorter |
| **Router**     | Moves data between different networks           | Regional postal hub            |
| **Modem**      | Translates your home network signal to your ISP | The highway on-ramp            |
